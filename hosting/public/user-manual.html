Signing in for the first time

The login screen is the same for patients and doctors — the app reads your role from your account and opens the right dashboard automatically.

  1. Enter your email — use the exact address your clinic registered. It is not case-sensitive.
  2. Enter your temporary password — the one supplied by your doctor or clinic administrator.
  3. Tap Sign In — patients land on the Patient Home screen, doctors on the Doctor Dashboard.
  4. Change your password — open Profile → Change Password straight away and set a private one.
First launch: a guided onboarding tour runs automatically and highlights each part of the screen. You can replay it any time with the ? button in the top bar.
Requirements: RehabCardia runs on Android 8.0 and above, and needs an internet connection to sync your exercises, feedback and messages.

Reading your home screen

  1. Today's Progress ring — the teal card fills as you complete each assignment for the day. The caption counts completed exercises, and the pill underneath warns you when your current plan is about to expire.
  2. Navigation cards — six shortcuts: Exercises, Schedule, Progress, Messages, Notifications and Profile. The red badge on Exercises is the number still outstanding today; it disappears once everything is done.

Your assignment list

  1. Category filters — narrow the list down to one type of exercise when you have several assignments running.
  2. Session counter — 1/3 means you have finished one of the three sessions prescribed for today. Tap the card to start the next one.
  3. Completed today — a green tick and a DONE pill mean this assignment is finished for the day. It resets automatically tomorrow.
Assignments only appear between the start and end dates your doctor set. If a plan expires, ask your doctor to extend or replace it.

The workout player

Starting an exercise opens the guided player. It loops through three phases for every set — DEMO, GO and REST — so you always know what to do next. Try it below: the buttons work exactly like the app.

How a session runs

  1. DEMO — the blue tag marks the demonstration phase. Watch the video your doctor attached, then tap I'm Ready.
  2. GO — the tag turns green. Perform the reps at your own pace and tap Set Done when you finish.
  3. REST — a countdown runs for the rest period your doctor prescribed, with audio cues in the last three seconds. Tap Skip Rest to move on early.
  4. Repeat — the dots above the rep counter fill in as each set completes. After the last set the session is saved and the feedback form opens.
Closing the player mid-session asks "End workout?". Choosing End abandons the session and it will not count towards today's progress.

Post-Workout Feedback

Tell your care team how the session felt

As soon as the last set is done, the feedback form opens. It takes under a minute and goes straight to your doctor — it is the main way they know whether your programme is working. The form below is live; try it.

What each field means

  1. Pain — yes or no. Answering Yes reveals the location chips so you can point your doctor at exactly where it hurt.
  2. Borg scales — two 0–10 sliders. The first rates how much it hurt, the second how hard your breathing was. The number changes colour as it climbs: green, amber, then red.
  3. Pulse rate — optional but useful. Measure your pulse right after the session; the app accepts 30–250 bpm.

Notes are free text and go into the same feedback thread your doctor reads, so anything unusual — dizziness, an ache the next day, a missed session — belongs here.

Stop and get help immediately if you have chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting or dizziness during or after exercise. Call your local emergency number — do not wait for a reply in the app.

Reading your charts

  1. Completion donut — the share of prescribed sessions you finished, partly finished, or missed over the selected period. Compliance is the number your doctor watches most.
  2. Weekly bars — teal is what you actually completed, grey is the target set by your assignment. A short teal bar next to a full grey one marks a day worth catching up on.
  • Session history — every workout is logged with its date, the exercise, sets completed, and both total and active duration.
  • Health metrics — your Borg pain and respiration scores are plotted over time so improvements are easy to see.
  • Streaks — consecutive days with all assignments completed.
Your doctor sees these same charts from their side, which is why honest feedback after each session matters more than a perfect-looking score.

Booking and tracking visits

  1. Week strip — tap any day to see its appointments. A small dot underneath a date means something is booked that day.
  2. Appointment cards — time, purpose, doctor and location, plus a status pill: Pending, Confirmed, Scheduled, Completed or Cancelled.

Messaging Your Doctor

Secure, in-app, non-urgent conversation

Messages sit in one continuous thread with your assigned doctor. They see it alongside your feedback and session history, so you rarely need to explain the context.

Using the chat

  • One thread per patient — everything stays in the same conversation, so nothing gets lost between visits.
  • Delivered in real time — new messages arrive as push notifications even when the app is closed.
  • Green dot beside your doctor's name means they are currently active in the app.
  • Good things to send: questions about an exercise, a change in symptoms, a session you had to skip and why.
Chat is not monitored around the clock. For anything urgent, contact your clinic by phone or call your local emergency number.

Type into the demo and press Enter — your message is added to the thread exactly as it would appear in the app.

The patient list

  1. Search and filter — find a patient by name or condition, or filter straight to the group you want to work through.
  2. Status at a glance — On Track, Needs Attention or Not Assigned, calculated from recent adherence. A green dot on the avatar means the patient is in the app right now.

Inside a patient profile

  • Assignments — every active prescription with live completion progress; edit or delete any of them.
  • Session history — each workout with sets completed, total duration and active duration.
  • Health metrics — Borg pain and respiration trends charted over the programme.
  • Care status override — force On Track or Needs Attention, or leave it on Auto.
  • Open chat — message the patient without leaving their record.
Adding a patient: fill in name, email, age, gender, phone and condition. A temporary password is generated automatically and the patient is assigned to you — pass those credentials on securely.

Exercise Library

The catalogue you prescribe from

Browsing and building the catalogue

  • Search and category chips — filter by cardio, stretching, strength, balance and so on.
  • Difficulty badges — Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced, shown on each thumbnail.
  • Preview before prescribing — tap any exercise to open the video in a modal player; MP4, MOV and YouTube links are all supported.
  • Upload your own — add a name, video file (up to 500 MB), category, difficulty, duration and instructions.
  • Delete — remove exercises you uploaded that are no longer in use.
Uploads happen in the background but large files still need a solid connection. Keep clips short and clearly framed — patients watch them on a phone before every set.

Assigning Exercises

Turning a library exercise into a patient's programme

Assignments are what the patient actually sees. Open a patient, pick an exercise, then set the dose — sets, reps, how many sessions a day, how long to rest, and the date range the plan runs for. The controls below behave like the real form.

Setting the dose

  1. Sets and reps — what the patient performs in a single session. These drive the set dots and rep counter in the workout player.
  2. Sessions per day and rest — up to three sessions daily, with a fixed rest countdown between sets. The patient's progress ring only fills once every session for the day is done.
  3. Plan period — the assignment appears in the patient's list only between these dates, and the app warns them as the end date approaches.

Instructions are shown with the exercise, so use them for form cues and stopping rules rather than general advice.

Saving sends the patient a push notification immediately. Editing or deleting an assignment later takes effect on their next refresh.

Reviewing Feedback

Where clinical signal arrives

Feedback and chat, side by side

  1. Feedback tab — a reverse-chronological timeline of post-workout submissions. Each entry carries pain yes/no, pain location, Borg pain and respiration scores, pulse rate and any notes typed by the patient.
  2. Chat tab — the same thread the patient sees, so you can respond to a worrying entry without switching screens.
  3. Session history — the objective counterpart: date, exercise, sets completed, total and active duration.

The Feedbacks card on the dashboard lists patients by most recent submission, so the newest clinical information is always at the top.

Entries reporting chest pain or a high respiration score deserve a same-day response. The app records feedback — it does not triage it.
